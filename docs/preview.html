Tone and treatment: badge

A tone sets four custom properties; a treatment reads them. Independent axes, so adding either is one rule rather than a matrix. Badge declares filled and outline only, and a badge is a label — these are not targets and carry no height floor.

This path was described here and never rendered, and it was broken: .dialog:not(dialog) weighs (0,1,1) because :not() takes its argument's specificity, which beat .dialog-fixed at (0,1,0) in the same layer. Keys and rules

Two classes that exist because composing them from memory is the failure mode. .kbd is a key cap on the label ramp, not the body one, so it sits in a sentence without changing its leading. .separator is a divider, and the decision it carries is which boundary role to take — --outline-subtle, what a page draws between two things, rather than --outline, what a control draws around itself. Selection controls

The box, the tick and the circle are the browser's, pointed at the brand with accent-color; the ladder sets the size and nothing else. That is what buys native indeterminate, native focus and a control that needs no markup contract — and it is what costs a custom radius and a custom unchecked fill. The switch has no native counterpart to borrow, so the class layer draws it: a track, and a thumb that is a radial gradient taking currentColor, which is why it slides and crossfades rather than snapping.

Every one of them is inside its label, and that is the target. An <input> is a replaced element and renders no ::before, so the hit area an icon-only button gets is not available here. The label is what a finger lands on, which is markup no stylesheet can supply for you.
